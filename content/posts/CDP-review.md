### 21-02-2023 / 23 Year Old
Surreal. Thinking back on this cert, there are quite a few funny memories too :))

Certified DevSecOps Professional (CDP) [Badge here!](https://www.credly.com/badges/bc4d98aa-0752-478e-99e0-57986ab9e8b5)

Hold on, let me say a little about it:

*The CDP credential validates the practical expertise to understand, implement and manage the DevSecOps program in an organization. A CDP can assess the current state of DevSecOps, embed security as part of DevOps, manage vulnerabilities and improve the overall Maturity Level. CDP holders can also identify gaps in secure SDLC implementation, Implement security as part of DevOps using Software Component Analysis, Static Analysis, Dynamic Analysis tools, Infrastructure as Code & Compliance as code.*

That's me copying it in full from their homepage. But honestly, to sum it all up, it's building a CI/CD pipeline that includes every bit of Software Component Analysis, Static Analysis, Dynamic Analysis tools, Infrastructure as Code & Compliance as code to keep an entire software development life cycle (SDLC – Software Development Life Cycle) secure.

Alright, let me share a few of my impressions of it. For those of you who want to take it, something to consider!
### The good
- DevOps and DevSecOps are making huge waves these days. So I wanted to take it too, to experience and learn more about DevOps, DevSecOps. By and large it also provides a pretty good general overview of knowledge.
- The materials and the learning labs are quite solid. Anyone who's taken Offensive's courses will know you need to connect to a VPN to do the labs. With CDP you just log into its web platform and you can get going right away, and the speed is fast and stable too.
- The labs are detailed, clearly explained.

### The limitations
- The course doesn't provide knowledge that's too deep
- The exam isn't too hard, not as tricky as Offensive's. I consider this a limitation because I like a bit of a challenge.
- Writing the report is a bit long :((

### A bit of rambling
When you register for the course you get 2 months to study and 1 month to do the labs. But during the exam the lab gets locked. Every task in the lab comes with a walkthrough. The exam isn't much harder than the tasks if you study carefully. Since it's locked during the exam, remember to take careful notes. Otherwise, in the exam there's nowhere left to copy, paste :)). The exam gives you 12h to do it and 24h to write the report.

Somewhere around 2 weeks was what I used to both study and do the labs. I think you'll be quick too if you have a bit of a base. But the annoying thing was, after finishing I couldn't book an exam slot because they were all full. That was right before Tet, so it just slipped by and I went home to celebrate Tet. After Tet I took the exam on the 14th of the lunar month. Almost a month without touching it, I thought I'd forgotten everything. And yet I finished the exam in just over 3h and sat writing the ridiculously long report in 6-7h. I submitted the report and got the pass notice 3 days after that.
First thing after the pass notice: I was out of pocket treating the guys at the company to a chicken buffet :))

Either way it's a fun memory, even though it wasn't as try-hard as OSCP and OSWE :))

I don't think I'll take the CDE (Certified DevSecOps Expert). OSEP -> the next target!
